## 1. Problem

Road Protect's appeal pipeline produces **one** template draft for every traffic-camera fine, regardless of which enforcement technology generated it. Israeli enforcement uses at least four distinct camera technologies — Gatso A-3, LTI 20-20 (ממל"ז), Bee III (דבורה), and red-light cameras — each with materially different legal failure modes and case law.

The most concrete example: in April 2025 the Ashdod court ruled that A-3 readings must be reduced by 9 km/h and that selective enforcement only on combustion vehicles (because EVs interfere with the measurement) is discriminatory. Every A-3 fine since that ruling is appealable on those grounds — but our current template does not invoke them.

**Who has the problem**: every Road Protect user with an open camera-issued fine. **How often**: every fine, every day. **What they do today**: get a generic draft, submit it, get the same acceptance rate the rest of the market gets.

## 2. Target user

**P2 — The frustrated fresh-fine recipient** (see `06_bot/personas/PERSONAS.md`), specifically narrowed to:

> A VIP subscriber or one-off appeal buyer who has just received a fine from an **automated enforcement device** (not an officer-stopped citation), where the fine was uploaded to RP within 30 days of issuance.

This excludes parking fines (different appeal logic) and officer-stopped tickets (witness-based).

## 3. The hypothesis

We believe that **generating a camera-type-specific appeal draft instead of a generic one** will cause **higher appeal acceptance rates** for **VIP and one-off appeal users**.

We'll know we're right if **acceptance rate uplifts by ≥5 percentage points vs. control** within **90 days of full rollout**.

## 4. Success metric (the one number)

**Appeal acceptance rate** for camera-issued fines processed through RP.

**Definition**: of N appeals submitted by users (with RP-generated drafts) in a 30-day cohort, the % where the issuing authority cancelled the fine or reduced its severity (lower amount, removed points). Measured per cohort, attributed to the date the user submitted.

**Baseline**: TBD. ⚠️ This is the #1 unblocker — we need it from RP ops or `09_metrics/` before we can set a real target.

**Target**: baseline + 5–10 pp uplift, sustained over two 30-day cohorts post-launch.

## 5. Secondary metrics & guardrails

- **Secondary 1**: time-to-draft (the OCR + classifier must not slow the pipeline below current SLA — target ≤30 seconds per fine end-to-end).
- **Secondary 2**: % of fines classified as "Unknown" (must be <15% — above that, the classifier is too weak to use).
- **Secondary 3**: human-reviewer override rate (must be <10% after first 200 reviewed — above that, the classifier is wrong too often).
- **Guardrail**: NO drop in acceptance rate vs. baseline. If A/B shows the new templates underperform the old generic one, we kill or revise.
- **Guardrail**: legal disclaimer must remain intact in every template variant (`01_business_context/LEGAL_DISCLAIMER.md`).

## 7. Requirements

### Must have (v1 ships without these = no go)
- [ ] **R1** OCR/NLP pipeline extracting: camera type, location text, measured speed, speed limit, date/time, fine amount, violation type, from uploaded ticket images and PDFs.
- [ ] **R2** Classifier mapping extracted text → camera-type label with ≥85% accuracy on a labeled test set of ≥200 real RP fines.
- [ ] **R3** Four new appeal templates (A-3, ממל"ז, דבורה, red-light) reviewed and approved by RP's legal/expert team.
- [ ] **R4** EV/hybrid cross-reference — read user's registered vehicle make/model and flag if applicable.
- [ ] **R5** A/B framework — randomly assign incoming camera fines to control (current template) or treatment (new pipeline). Outcome tracked per cohort.
- [ ] **R6** Human-reviewer override UI for the RP appeal-handling team.
- [ ] **R7** Outcome tracking — appeal-acceptance status backfilled into our system (manual entry by the team is fine for v1).

### Should have (v1.1 or fast-follow)
- [ ] **R8** Confidence score on the classifier output exposed to the reviewer ("we're 92% sure this is A-3").
- [ ] **R9** Reviewer feedback loop — overrides used as labeled data to retrain.
- [ ] **R10** Notification to the user explaining *why* their appeal is strong (transparency = trust = retention).

### Won't have (this round)
- **R11** Building a camera-location database with per-camera calibration history — Phase 2.
- **R12** Auto-requesting "device card" from police prosecution — Phase 3.
- **R13** Hebrew-English bilingual templates — single Hebrew templates only.
- **R14** Templates for fines from authorities outside police + the ~22 covered municipalities.

## 8. Edge cases & error states

- **Camera type not explicit on the ticket** → classifier returns "Unknown" → fallback to current generic template. Don't fail, don't surface uncertainty to the user.
- **OCR fails / unreadable scan** → ask user to re-upload with a clearer photo. Don't auto-generate from garbage input.
- **Multiple violations on one ticket** → one appeal per violation, each independently classified.
- **A-3 fine + EV/hybrid but vehicle make/model not in user profile** → still classify as A-3, just no special badge. Don't block.
- **User uploads a fine from before April 2025 and it's an A-3** → still apply the new template (the ruling applies retroactively to open fines; the appeal stands or falls on the legal argument, not when the fine was issued).
- **Mid-flight ticket: user uploads while classifier is being changed** → freeze template assignment at upload time; don't re-classify after submission.
- **Adversarial user uploads fake fine** → out of scope for v1; ops has a manual review step before drafts are sent.

## 9. Legal / compliance review

This PRD changes the *substance* of the legal argument we generate. Sensitivities:

1. **The legal disclaimer must be preserved in every template variant.** The disclaimer in `01_business_context/LEGAL_DISCLAIMER.md` is non-negotiable. Each new template will be reviewed against it.
2. **The Ashdod ruling citation must be accurate.** We cite a specific court ruling (April 2025, Ashdod). Wrong citation = our credibility collapses + risk of opposing-counsel discrediting in court. Legal team must verify the case reference before any A-3 template ships.
3. **The EV/hybrid argument must not be over-claimed.** The court found "likely" interference, not "proven." Template must use accurate hedged language.
4. **We are not lawyers.** "עורך דין ה-AI" framing in the bot is the line. Templates must stay as drafts; user submits.
5. **Outcome tracking & data retention.** Adding a structured outcome field to the user record. Confirm with Privacy that this is covered under existing T&C; if not, T&C update required before launch.

## 10. Rollout plan

**Stage 0 — Internal validation (week 1–2)**
- Re-process 50 historical fines (closed cases) with the new pipeline.
- Human reviewer compares the new draft to the original. Flag failures.
- Gate to Stage 1: ≥90% of historical re-runs produce a draft the reviewer would have sent.

**Stage 1 — Soft launch (week 3–6)**
- A/B at 10% of new camera fines (50/50 within that 10%).
- Track classifier "Unknown" rate, OCR success rate, reviewer override rate, draft generation time.
- Gate to Stage 2: secondary-metric guardrails all green, no legal incidents.

**Stage 2 — 50% rollout (week 6–8)**
- Acceptance-rate uplift is the primary signal here. Need at least one 30-day cohort.
- Gate to Stage 3: acceptance rate ≥ control, with a positive trend.

**Stage 3 — Full launch (week 8+)**
- 100% of camera fines through new pipeline.
- Measurement continues for 90 days; declare success or revise.

**Rollback plan**: classifier output is a feature flag. Flipping it back to "use generic template" reverts the system. No data migration required.
